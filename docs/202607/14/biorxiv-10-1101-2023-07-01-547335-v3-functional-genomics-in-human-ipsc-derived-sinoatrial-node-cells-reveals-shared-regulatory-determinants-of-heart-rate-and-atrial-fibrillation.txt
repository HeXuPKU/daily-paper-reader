ABSTRACT 30 

Human model systems for functional genomics of heart rhythm are needed to translate 31 

genome wide association studies into biological insight and actionable targets. Here we 32 

develop a human induced pluripotent stem cell sinoatrial node system that recapitulated 33 

the transcriptional and epigenetic heterogeneity of primary human pacemaker tissue, 34 

permitting exploration of heart rhythm-associated single nucleotide polymorphisms 35 

(SNPs) in a cell subtype-specific manner. Using self-transcribing active regulatory region 36 

sequencing (STARR-seq), we experimentally validated numerous enhancers containing 37 

heart rhythm associated variants. We demonstrated the utility of this platform for fine 38 

mapping of candidate causal SNPs by identifying an AF-associated variant at the ATXN1 39 

locus that affects signal responsiveness of an enhancer, and a variant at the GNB4 locus 40 

that regulates cardiac autonomic sensitivity, leading to a pleiotropic effect on heart rate 41 

and atrial fibrillation. Taken together, these data establish a robust human cellular system 42 

to explore the mechanistic basis of heart rhythm heritability. 43

INTRODUCTION 45 

Pacemaker cardiomyocytes (PCs) in the sinoatrial node (SAN) generate a46 

synchronized electrical wavefront that initiates the heartbeat 1. PCs are integrated with the 47 

autonomic nervous system and have a complex functional and anatomical organization, 48 

allowing the SAN to achieve beat-to-beat modulation of heart rate to match changes in 49 

metabolic demand 2,3 . The SAN head, located at the junction of the right atrium and 50 

superior vena cava, is most sensitive to autonomic input, driving impulse generation 51 

under high sympathetic tone 4,5 . The more inferiorly located SAN tail stabilizes basal 52 

automaticity when the SAN head is inhibited by high parasympathetic tone, while a 53 

transition zone at the interface of the SAN and atrial myocardium promotes unidirectional 54 

impulse transmission from the SAN to the atrium. 2,6,7 

55 

SAN dysfunction (SND), among the most common heart rhythm disorders, causes 56 

slow heartbeat leading to a need for pacemaker implantation. While it is well established 57 

that SAN head, SAN tail, and atrial-like transitional cells engage different transcriptional 58 

networks, 7-10 the detailed mechanisms that drive cell subtype-specific gene programs 59 

underlying normal SAN function and SND are incompletely understood. Heart rate is a 60 

strongly heritable trait 11 , and genome wide association studies have defined hundreds of 61 

loci associated with heart rate determination and susceptibility to SND, 12,13 providing a 62 

potential window into the cellular and molecular basis for SAN function and SND. 63 

However, the cellular subtypes and specific contexts in which common variants regulate 64 

heart rhythm and disease susceptibility are still not well-established, partly due to the lack 65 

of suitable experimental model systems. 66 

Furthermore, SND and slow heart rate are associated with a higher incidence of 67 

atrial fibrillation (AF) 14 , a distinct arrhythmia in which ectopic firing in the atrium triggers 68 

chaotic electrical activity and rapid heartbeat. Despite their apparent dissimilarity, SAN 69 

dysfunction and AF frequently occur in the same individual 15 , and are often presumed to 70 

reflect sequalae of a shared underlying fibrotic atrial substrate. 16,17 However, both GWAS 71 

and Mendelian Randomization have suggested a deeper mechanistic connection 72 

between SAN function and AF 18-20 , in which common variants associated with slower 73 

resting heart rate may also drive a higher incidence of AF as has been observed for a 74 

rare variant at the PITX2 locus 21 . Determining whether slower heartbeat causes AF, or 75

Human induced pluripotent stem cells (hiPSCs) can be differentiated into various 79 

cardiomyocyte subtypes, including atrial 22 , ventricular 23 , and SAN 24-26 . PC subtypes were 80 

resolved from SAN differentiations using single cell (sc)-RNA sequencing and clustering 81 

analysis 27 , raising the possibility of using in vitro systems to model specific common 82 

variants associated with heart rhythm in the cell subtypes of interest, to gain insights into 83 

the pathogenesis of arrhythmia, and to identify mechanisms that mediate the connection 84 

between SND and atrial fibrillation. More fundamentally, common variants are presumed 85 

to act by affecting gene expression through cis-regulatory elements 28,29 , so a model 86 

system that permits systematic interrogation of the regulatory architecture of SAN cell 87 

types could identify transcriptional mechanism that control cell subtype specialization. 88 

Here we used a scalable monolayer hiPSC SAN differentiation protocol to yield 89 

SAN head, SAN tail, atrial-like transition zone cells, and sinus venosus myocardium. 90 

Single cell (sc)-RNA-seq and sc-ATAC-seq defined the transcriptomic and epigenetic 91 

signatures of these cell subtypes, established their fidelity to their in vivo counterparts, 92 

and identified transcriptional networks engaged by different cell subtypes. Next, by using 93 

parallel reporter assays and leveraging allele-specific chromatin accessibility, we 94 

identified many novel SAN enhancers, including several that contained causative SNPs. 95 

These data provide insight into the mechanisms of trait heritability and define a role for 96 

genetically encoded autonomic responsiveness in mediating the association of sinus 97 

node function and AF. 98

DISCUSSION 497 

In this work, we established that hiPSC-derived SANCMs recapitulate the cellular 498 

diversity observed in the human adult SAN and used this system to interrogate heritable 499 

variation in regulation of heart rate and susceptibility to atrial fibrillation. Our findings 500 

implicate cardiac inherited autonomic responsiveness as a mechanism that connects 501 

sinus node function to arrhythmia susceptibility, providing a clear example of the utility of 502 

this approach to elucidate mechanistic relationships among closely related cardiovascular 503 

traits. 504

HiPSC SAN Cultures for Functional Genomics 557 

Common variants are theorized to influence traits by directly affecting the activities 558 

of cis -regulatory elements that regulate expression of nearby genes within functionally 559 

relevant cell types 59 ,60 . Consistent with this framework, we found that differentially 560 

accessible chromatin in each of the SANCM subtypes significantly overlapped with SNPs 561 

associated with cardiac traits such as resting heart rate (SAN tail and transitional cells > 562 

SAN head) and heart rate recovery after exercise (SAN head > SAN tail). This finding is 563 

supports the model of functional compartmentalization, in which migration of the leading 564 

pacemaker along the cranial-caudal dimension is driven by changes in autonomic tone 565 

that affects SAN head cells disproportionately, whereas resting heart rate is driven by 566 

mechanisms of impulse generation and transmission that depend on function of the SAN 567 

tail and transitional cells. 568

a well- established link between sinus node function and the propensity to develop AF 61 .570

Role of Autonomic Sensitivity in Mediating the Association of Sinus Node Function 671 

and Atrial Fibrillation 672 

Genes required for mediating autonomic responsiveness of the SAN are highly 673 

enriched among GWAS hits for heart rate determination and heart rate response to 674 

exercise 77 . Not surprisingly, we found that scATAC-seq peaks at many of these loci 675 

contained SNPs associated with heart rate. Furthermore, the association of scATAC-seq 676 

peaks from all myocardial cell types with atrial fibrillation risk suggested that the SANCM 677 

platform might offer a means to explore the genetic contribution to the association 678 

between sinus node function and atrial fibrillation. 679 

It is well established that vagal input to the atrium and pulmonary venous 680 

myocardium can facilitate atrial fibrillation through IKACh, an outward hyperpolarizing 681 

current that is activated by G bg in response to muscarinic agonism by acetyl choline or 682 

local adenosine release 78,79 . In atrial myocardium, IKACh activation leads to action 683 

potential shortening and thereby to a shorter refractory period and an improved safety 684 

factor for conduction, both of which are pro-fibrillatory changes in tissue 685 

electrophysiology. Indeed, the drug Tertiapin, a specific IKACh blocker, is effective for 686 

termination of atrial fibrillation episodes in some contexts 80 . In the SAN, the same 687 

pathway is operative, but the hyperpolarization caused by IKACh leads to a reduction in 688 

the slope of diastolic depolarization and a lower maximum diastolic potential, both of 689 

which reduce automaticity 81,82 . Thus, a heritable increase in cardiac vagal sensitivity 690 

would, in theory, result in a higher risk of atrial fibrillation and a lower heart rate. 691
