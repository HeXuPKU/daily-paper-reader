Abstract 14 

Drought during reproductive development and grain filling is a major constraint to bread 15 

wheat productivity in rainfed environments. In the present study, we employed genome - 16 

wide association analysis in an untapped diversity panel of wheat genotypes relevant to 17 

natural dryland conditions. A total of 186 genotypes were evaluated for drought -related 18 

physiological, biomass, and architectural traits under terminal rainfed stress in Azerbai- 19 

jan. Relative wat er content, plant height, fresh weight, dry weight, flag leaf length, and 20 

flag leaf width were assessed at the milk ripening stage. These data were su bjected to 21 

genome -wide association analysis using 19,737 SNP markers to identify loci and epistatic 22 

intera ctions involved in the determination of these traits. The panel showed broad phe- 23 

notypic variation and significant genotypic effects for all traits, wi th broad -sense herita- 24 

bility ranging from 0.991 for plant height to 0.385 for flag leaf width. GWAS identif ied a 25 

major locus for relative water content on chromosome 2D at SNP marker AX -86184518, 26 

which explained 11.94% of the genotypic variation. Candidate -gene analysis highlighted 27 

the proximal WEB -family -like gene TraesCS2D03G1001000 as the main candidate gene . 28 

Plant height showed strong additive loci, mainly on chromosomes 2A and 4A, whereas 29 

biomass and flag leaf traits showed suggestive additive loci and epistatic interactions. 30 

These findings provide candidate loci and interaction patterns in the genetic make -up of 31 

essential traits, which may facilitate indirect selection in breeding new varieties. 32 

Keywords: Triticum aestivum L.; terminal drought; GWAS; ep istasis; RWC; plant height; 33 

flag leaf morphology; candidate genes 34

## 1. Introduction 36 

Bread wheat ( Triticum a estivum L.) is central to global food security because it con- 37 

tributes substantially to dietary energy, plant -based protein and other nutritional com- 38 

po nents in human diets (Poole et al., 2021). Recent FAOSTAT data indicate that global 39 

wheat production remai ns close to 800 million tonnes annually from more than 200 mil- 40 

lion hectares, confirming wheat as one of the most widely grown food crops worldwide 41 

(FA O, 2026). Maintaining this production level under changing climatic conditions is 42

conseq uences for rainfed production systems (IPCC, 2023; He et al., 2024). Because a large 45 

share of global wheat is produced under rainfed conditions, yield stability is strongly 46 

influenced by the timing, duration and intensity of water deficit (Dadrasi et al., 2023). 47 

Drought occurring during reproductive development and grain filling is especially dam- 48 

aging, as it r estricts canopy function, accelerates senescence and reduces assimilate avail- 49 

ability for grain formation (Farooq et al., 2014; Ahmed et al., 2022). 50 

Dr ought reduces wheat performance through a sequence of linked physiological 51 

and growth responses, beginning with impaired plant water status and progressing to 52 

reduced carbon assimilation, canopy development and biomass accumulation. Leaf rel- 53 

ative water con tent (RWC) provides a direct estimate of leaf hydration because it relates 54 

fresh tissue mass to fully turg id and oven -dry mass (Turner, 1981). In bread wheat, recent 55 

morpho -physiological screening under well -watered and drought -stressed conditions 56 

confirme d that RWC varies significantly among genotypes and declines under water def- 57 

icit, while its association wi th excised leaf water retention, relative water loss and mem- 58 

brane stability reflects broader differences in leaf water status and drought response (Se- 59 

wore et al., 2023). Water -relation analyses in contrasting wheat genotypes further indi- 60 

cate that drought r esponsiveness depends on the coordination between leaf water status, 61 

stomatal regulation, osmotic adjustment and cell wall elasticity rather than on l eaf water 62 

content alone (Guizani et al., 2023). Thus, RWC, fresh weight (FW) and dry weight (DW) 63 

provide c omplementary information on the physiological and growth components of 64 

drought response, making them suitable traits for genetic analysis under field moisture 65 

limitation. 66 

Plant architecture is another important component of dryland adaptation because it 67 

in fluences lodging resistance, biomass distribution, harvest index and suitability to tar- 68 

get production environments. Plant height (PH) is not simply a trait to be reduced: ex- 69 

cessively tall plants are more prone to lodging, whereas excessively short plants m ay 70 

show reduced biomass and yield potential (Zhang et al., 2025). Recent genetic analysis of 71 

plant -height -related traits in wheat also showed that dry land cultivars were approxi- 72 

mately 10 cm taller than irrigated cultivars, supporting the view that optimal plant ar- 73 

chitecture may differ between water -limited and irrigated environments (Zhang et al., 74 

2025). The Green Revolution semi -dwarfing alleles Rht -B1 b and Rht -D1b improved lodg- 75 

ing resistance and harvest index, but gibberellin -insensitive reduced -height al leles can 76 

shorten coleoptiles and reduce early seedling vigor, which may be disadvantageous un- 77 

der dry sowing or deep -sowing conditions (Rebetzke and R ichards, 2000; Hao et al., 78 

2023). Alternative reduced -height loci therefore remain relevant for dryland br eeding, 79 

particularly when moderate height reduction is needed without compromising early es- 80 

tablishment; Rht25 , for example, has been characterized as a gibberellin -sensitive dwarf- 81 

ing locus affecting PH in wheat (Mo et al., 2018). In addition to plant heigh t, flag leaf 82 

length (FLL) and flag leaf width (FLW) are agronomically relevant because the flag leaf 83 

is a major photosynthetic source during grain fil ling. Genetic dissection of flag leaf mor- 84 

phology under contrasting water regimes has shown that these trai ts are quantitatively 85 

inherited and responsive to water availability (Yang et al., 2016). Flag -leaf -related traits, 86

The genetic dissection of drought -re lated traits is complicated by their quantitative 94 

inheritance and sensitivity to environmental conditions. Drought adaptation in wheat is 95 

generally co ntrolled by multiple physiological, developmental and genetic mechanisms 96 

rather than by single major -effec t loci (Sallam et al., 2019). Biparental quantitative trait 97 

locus (QTL) mapping has been useful for detecting segregating loci in specific popula- 98 

tion s, but its allelic coverage is limited by the two parental genotypes used in each cross. 99 

Genome -wide assoc iation stady (GWAS) provides a complementary approach because 100 

it exploits historical recombination and linkage disequilibrium in diverse panels to detect 101 

marker –trait associations at higher resolution. In wheat, GWAS has been used to identify 102 

loci associat ed with seedling dr ought tolerance (Maulana et al., 2020), osmotic adjust- 103 

ment and related drought -adaptive traits in durum wheat (Condorelli et al., 2022), and 104 

drought -induced proline and hydrogen peroxide accumulation under natural field and 105 

rain -out shel ter conditions (Kam ruzzaman et al., 2022). In Turkish winter wheat 106 

germplasm, the integration of GWAS and selective sweep analysis identified genomic 107 

regions and candidate genes associated with improved grain yield under drought con- 108 

ditions (Sehgal et al., 2024). The biologic al interpretation of these associations has been 109 

strengthened by the availability of annotated wheat genome resources, including the ref- 110 

erence genome and multi -genome assemblies (IWGSC, 2018; Walkowiak et al., 2020). 111 

More recent studies have identified sin gle -nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) markers, 112 

candidate genes and gene networks associated with drought tolerance and drought -re- 113 

sponse indices in wheat (Nouraei et al., 2024; Mosalam et al., 2025; Mourad et al., 2026). 114 

However, most GWAS st udies still emphasi ze additive marker effects, whereas non -ad- 115 

ditive marker interactions may also contribute to trait variation under field stress. There- 116 

fore, combining additive GWAS with epistatic interaction analysis may provide a more 117 

complete view of th e genetic basis of drought -related variation in adapted wheat 118 

germplasm. 119 

Despite increasing progress in wheat drought genetics, important knowledge gaps 120 

remain for rainfed field environments. Many studies have used seedling -stage assays, 121 

hydroponic or PEG -induced stress, or controlled drought treatments that do not fully 122 

reproduce the fluctuating moisture availability, temperature conditions and soil con- 123 

straints experienced in dryland production systems. In addition, the genetic coordination 124 

among foliar hy dration, biomass ac cumulation and plant architecture remains insuffi- 125 

ciently understood in locally and regionally adapted germplasm evaluated under real 126 

field moisture limitation. Addressing this gap is important because drought adaptation 127 

in breeding progr ams depends not onl y on statistically significant markers, but also on 128 

markers associated with biologically interpretable traits expressed in target production 129 

environments. 130

Being integral geographic part of wheat center of diversity and origin, the drylan d 131 

conditions of Azerbaijan provide a relevant field context for rainfed wheat production 132 

environment globally. Under these natural dryland conditions, the production is ex- 133 

posed to variable precipitation and terminal -season moisture limitation, while local and 134 

regionally adapted germplasm may retain allelic variation shaped by recurrent water - 135 

limited conditions (Red Cross Red Crescent Climate Centre, 2024). Previous studies on 136 

Azerbaijani wheat germplasm have revealed substantial variation in drought -respon si ve 137 

physiological, biochemical, ultrastructural and molecular traits (Rustamova et al. 2021; 138 

Aliyeva et al., 2023; Allahverdiyev, 2024; Isgandarova et al., 2024; Allahverdiyev et al. 139 

2025). However, the genome -wide basis of this adaptive variation remains insufficiently 140 

characterized under rainfed field conditions. 141 

The present study evaluated a diverse panel of 186 bread wheat genotypes under 142 

rainfed field conditions in Azerbaijan. The objectives were to: (1) characterize phenotypic 143 

variation, broad -sense he ritability and inter -trait relationships for RWC, PH, FW, DW, 144 

FLL and FLW under rainfed field conditions; (2) identify additive marker –trait associa- 145 

tions using high -density SNP -based GWAS; (3) evaluate epistatic marker –marker inter- 146 

actions contributing to drought -related trait variation; and (4) identify candidate genes 147 

located near significant marker. By linking physiological, architectural and biomass traits 148 

with genome -wide marker information, this study aims to clarify the genetic basis of 149 

drought -rela te d variation in field -grown wheat and to support the identification of mark- 150 

ers and candidate genes relevant for breeding in water -limited environments. 151

2.1. Plant material 153 

The plant material utilized in the present study consist s of a newly established di- 154

2.2. Experimental design and growth condition 161 

Field experiments were conducted during the 2022/2023 growing season at the Go- 162 

bustan Regional Experimental Station of the Research Institute of Crop Husbandry, 163 

Azerbaijan (800 m a.s.l.). The soil in this region is classified as light chestnut with a 164 

slightly alkaline pH. It is characterized by low humus content (1.5 -1.8% in the topsoil), 165 

low nitrogen and phosphorus availability, and moderate potassium levels. The hydro - 166 

meteorological conditions for the 2022/2023 growing season are presented in detail in 167 

Supplementary Table 2. Total precipitation during the cropping season wa s 356.3 mm, 168 

which is below the long -term average of 406.0 mm. A critical moisture deficit occurred 169 

in June, with only 4.1 mm of rainfall accompanied by relatively high temperatures, caus- 170 

ing pronounced drought stress during the reproductive stages of wheat de velopment. 171 

Each experimental plot measured 1.5 × 1.0 m (1.5 m²), with rows spaced 15 cm apart. 172

2.3. Phenotypic evaluation of drought -related traits 176 

Phenotypic measurements were conducted during the first ten days of June 2023, at 177 

the milk ripeness stage. Six drought -related agronomic and physiological traits were 178 

evaluated: PH, FLL, FLW, FW, DW, and RWC. 179 

PH was measured from the soil surface to the top of the spike, excluding awns. FLL 180 

was measured from the base to the tip of the fully developed flag leaf, while FLW was 181 

measured at the widest part of the same leaf using a ruler (Yang et al., 2 016). 182 

For fresh and dry weight determination, leaf samples were collected from each gen- 183 

otype and replication. FW was recorded immediately after sampling. The samples were 184 

then dried in a thermostat at 80°C until constant weight, and DW was recorded. 185 

RWC w as measured according to the method proposed by Turner (1981). Fresh, me- 186 

dium -sized leaves were weighed immediately after collection to determine FW. The 187 

leaves were then immersed in distilled water for 24 h to reach full turgidity, after which 188 

turgid weigh t (TW) was recorded. Subsequently, the samples were dried at 80°C until 189 

constant weight and weighed again to determine DW. RWC was calculated using the 190 

following formula: 191 

RWC (%) = [(FW − DW) / (TW − DW)] × 100 192 

> 193

where FW is the initial fresh weight of the leaf, TW is the turgid weight after rehy- 194 

dration, and DW is the dry weight after oven -drying. 195

modelling strategy described by Benaouda et al. (2022). To reduce the computational 242 

burden of testing all possible marker -by -mar ker combinations, only markers showing an 243 

initial single -marker association signal of P < 0.7 were ret ained for epistasis testing. 244 

Because the present study was conducted in a single field environment, the envi- 245 

ronmental interaction term was not included in the model. The following model was 246 

used: 247

genoty pe classes, and εijk is the residual error. 253

panel were not controlled as a single uniform response, but rather as partly independent 549

relation analysis. Biomass traits were strongly associated with flag leaf morphology, 551 

whereas RWC showed only a limited association with biomass and almost no relationship 552 

with PH. This suggests that the ability to maintain leaf hydration un der terminal r ainfed 553 

stress was not simply a consequence of larger plant size or greater vegetative growth. 554 

Similar conclusions have been drawn from physiological studies in wheat, where drought 555 

adaptation was shown to depend on coordinated but distinct me chanisms, incl uding wa- 556 

ter retention, osmotic adjustment, stomatal regulation, and maintenance of photosynthetic 557 

activity (Sallam et al., 2019; Guizani et al., 2023). 558 

The partial independence of RWC from plant height and dry biomass is important for 559 

interpr eting the GWAS results. RWC is commonly considered a direct indicator of leaf 560 

water status, but it is not necessarily expected to follow the same genetic architecture as 561 

growth -related traits. Condorelli et al. (2022) showed in durum wheat that osmotic adj ust- 562 

ment, RWC, and chlorophyll retention were physiologically connected under drought; 563 

however, their genetic control was distributed across several genomic regions. Similarly, 564 

Czyczyło -Mysza et al. (2018) showed that traits related to excised -leaf water lo ss are ge- 565 

netic ally complex and may overlap with leaf morphology without being simply deter- 566 

mined by leaf size. The strongest association for RWC was detected on chromosome 2D 567 

at AX -86184518. This locus is of particular interest because it passed the correct ed signifi- 568 

canc e threshold under rainfed field conditions, where physiological traits are usually af- 569 

fected by environmental heterogeneity. Previous studies have reported loci associated 570 

with RWC or water status on other chromosomes, including SSR markers on 5A and 5D 571 

in biparental wheat material (Naroui Rad et al., 2012), as well as drought -related loci on 572 

2A identified through association mapping (Ahmad et al., 2014). In contrast, the present 573 

association on chromosome 2D appears to represent a different gen omic region ex pressed 574 

under terminal field stress. Therefore, AX -86184518 can be considered a promising candi- 575 

date marker for leaf water -status maintenance in this germplasm. This is relevant because 576 

RWC is strongly influenced by developmental stage, stress intensity, an d genetic back- 577 

ground, as also shown in recent GWAS studies of wheat under drought, osmotic, and salt - 578 

related stress (Javid et al., 2022; Reddy et al., 2023; Nouraei et al., 2024). The candidate - 579 

gene content around AX -86184518 gives this locus additional bi ological interest. The near- 580 

est gene, TraesCS2D03G1001000, is annotated as an uncharacterized WEB -family -like pro- 581 

tein. WEB/PMI2 -related proteins have been studied in Arabidopsis as components in- 582 

volved in blue -light -induced chloroplast photorel ocation (Luess e et al., 2006; Kodama et 583 

al., 2010). This provides a plausible connection with chloroplast -associated stress re- 584 

sponses. Under terminal drought, leaves are exposed not only to water deficit, but also to 585 

high irradiance and oxidative pressure; therefore, ge nes related to chloroplast positioning 586 

or chloroplast protection represent biologically relevant positional candidates near a wa- 587 

ter -status locus. The neighbouring gene TraesCS2D03G1000800, annotated as a PEPCK - 588 

like protein, is also physiologi cally relevant . PEPCK -related enzymes participate in or- 589 

ganic acid metabolism and carbon remobilization, processes that may influence source – 590 

sink balance and stress responses (Lea et al., 2001; Yang et al., 2026). Together, these can- 591 

didate genes suggest tha t the 2D regio n may be connected with chloroplast function, car- 592 

bon metabolism, and stress -related physiological adjustment, although functional studies 593 

will be needed to define their direct roles in wheat drought response. 594 

PH showed a much clearer additive genetic patte rn than the other traits. Several 595 

markers passed the corrected threshold, especially on chromosomes 2A and 4A, indicat- 596 

ing that PH was under strong genetic control in this panel. This is consistent with previous 597 

wheat studies, where PH has rep eatedly been s hown to be a highly heritable trait con- 598 

trolled by loci affecting internode elongation, semi -dwarfing response, and overall plant 599 

architecture (Rebetzke and Richards, 2000; Mo et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2025). In the 600

dimensions. This suggests that, in this germplasm, height variation was largely independ- 602 

ent from leaf hydration and vegetative biomass formation. For breeding under rainfed 603 

conditions, this i s useful becau se plant architecture may be adjusted without necessarily 604 

changing water -status traits. The detected PH loci on 2A and 4A are also interesting in 605 

relation to previous mapping studies. Chromosome 2A has repeatedly been implicated in 606 

plant -heig ht variation i n wheat, including recent analyses of PH -related traits (Zhang et 607 

al., 2025). Chromosome 4A has also appeared in studies of drought - and heat -adaptive 608 

traits, where QTLs for canopy temperature and yield -related responses under stress were 609 

rep orted (Pinto e t al., 2010). Therefore, the present results support the general importance 610 

of these chromosomes for wheat architecture and stress -adaptive plant development. 611 

Biomass traits showed a different pattern. Although fresh and dry weight were 612 

close ly correlated and had relatively high heritability, they did not produce additive as- 613 

sociations passing the corrected multiple -testing thresholds. This suggests that biomass 614 

accumulation under terminal drought in this panel may not be controlled by one or t wo 615 

large -effec t loci. Instead, it may depend on many small -effect loci, environment -sensitive 616 

effects, or non -additive interactions. This agrees with the broader GWAS literature on 617 

wheat drought response, where terminal drought traits at the reproductive s tage often 618 

sho w complex and polygenic inheritance (Reddy et al., 2023; Nouraei et al., 2024). The 619 

shared suggestive association on chromosome 7A for FW and DW is therefore worth not- 620 

ing as a putative genomic region contributing to biomass accumulation under rainfed 621 

stres s. 622 

Flag leaf morphology was strongly associated with biomass at the phenotypic level. 623 

This is expected, because the flag leaf is an important photosynthetic source during repro- 624 

ductive development and grain filling. Yang et al. (2016) showed t hat flag leaf length, 625 

width, and area are quantitatively inherited and influenced by water regime, whereas 626 

Wang et al. (2022) reported that flag -leaf -related loci may affect yield -related traits. How- 627 

ever, in the present study, the GWAS evidence for flag le af traits remained mainly sug- 628 

gestive, supporting the view that flag leaf architecture is controlled by multiple loci and 629 

is sensitive to environmental conditions. 630 

The marker Kukri_c37738_417 on chromosome 1D deserves attention because it ap- 631 

peared as a shar ed sugges tive association for RWC, FW, DW, and FLL. Such a pattern 632 

may indicate a genomic region where leaf hydration, leaf expansion, and biomass accu- 633 

mulation are partially connected. Although this marker did not pass the corrected signif- 634 

icance thresholds , the 1D region may contain linked or pleiotropic variation affecting sev- 635 

eral drought -related traits. Further haplotype analysis would help clarify whether this as- 636 

sociation reflects biological pleiotropy or local linkage disequilibrium. 637 

The epistatic analy sis helpe d to explain why some traits with clear phenotypic varia- 638 

tion did not show strong additive GWAS associations. Putative marker -by -marker inter- 639 

actions were detected for all traits, with particularly relevant patterns for RWC and bio- 640 

mass traits. For F W and DW, where corrected additive associations were absent, the epi- 641 

stasis results suggest that allele combinations at two loci may contribute to biomass vari- 642 

ation under terminal stress. This agrees with the general view that drought adaptation in 643 

wheat in cludes bo th additive and non -additive genetic components (Sallam et al., 2019; 644 

Gupta et al., 2017). Therefore, the epistatic results complement the additive GWAS find- 645 

ings and provide an additional layer for interpreting the genetic architecture of drought - 646 

related t raits in this panel. 647

Overall, the study reveals three main genetic patterns. First, RWC was associated with a 649 

strong candidate locus on chromosome 2D, making AX -86184518 the most promising 650 

marker for leaf water -status maintenance in this pan el. Second, PH was controlled by 651 

several strong additive loci and was largely independent from RWC and biomass traits. 652 

Third, biomass and flag leaf traits were phenotypically connected but genetically more 653 

complex, with mainly suggestive additive markers a nd evidence of putative epistatic 654 

effects. These results are consistent with the current understanding of drought adapta- 655 

tion in wheat as a multi -component trait, where physiological water status, canopy ar- 656 

chitecture, and biomass formation should b e evaluat ed together rather than reduced to a 657 

single marker or trait. Future studies should focus on testing the stability of the 2D RWC 658 

locus and the major PH -associated loci across different years, locations, and drought 659 

scenarios. Integrating these loci with gra in yield, canopy temperature, osmotic adjust- 660 

ment, root traits, and high -throughput phenotyping indices will help to better under- 661 

stand their contribution to drought adaptation and support their potential use in breed- 662 

ing programs. 663
