Abstract 1

Causal variants of complex traits are enriched at transcription factor (TF) binding sites and are thought to 2

contribute to pathology by disrupting TF activity and thereby causing transcriptome dysregulation. However, 3

existing approaches typically address TF-mediated gene regulatory networks (TF-GRNs) and transcriptomes 4

separately, and methods that jointly leverage both to systematically assess disease heritability remain limited. 5

We aimed to develop a framework that jointly leverages TF-GRNs and transcriptomes to assess disease 6

heritability. Here, we constructed a matrix encoding TF-GRNs and developed an unsupervised analytical 7

pipeline, Canonical correlation Analysis of Transcriptome and TF-gene regulatory Networks (CATaN). CATaN 8

applies canonical correlation analysis (CCA) to extract canonical correlation (CC) components, i.e., shared 9

variation components between transcriptomes and TF-GRNs, and converts them into genome-wide functional 10 

annotation scores connected to stratified LD score regression (S-LDSC) for heritability analysis. We applied 11 

CATaN to eight datasets, including 19,198 bulk samples and 611,772 single cells from human and mouse 12 

sources, identifying 588 CC components that are significantly enriched for SNP heritability across 69 complex 13 

traits. Notably, functional annotation tracks based on these TF-GRNs are distinct from transcriptome signatures 14 

prioritized by LDSC-SEG, with greater heritability enrichment for a subset of traits. Finally, we suggest that 15 

CATaN may help prioritize candidate causal variants for experimental fine-mapping using genome editing. 16 

Together, integrating TF-GRNs with transcriptomes reveals disease-relevant regulatory programs that are not 17 

fully captured by transcriptome-based analyses alone. 18

Introduction 1

Genome-wide association studies (GWASs) indicate that complex diseases arise from the cumulative effects of 2

numerous causal variants 1,2,3 . Methods for partitioning SNP heritability, such as stratified LD score regression 3

(S-LDSC) 4 , quantify the enrichment of SNP heritability within defined genomic functional regions. Analyses of 4

publicly available TF chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) datasets 5 have revealed that 5

disease-associated heritability is concentrated within TF binding sites, for example, binding sites of NF-κB6

family members in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) 6 . These observations suggest that genetic variants perturb 7

TF-mediated regulatory programs and that TF gene regulatory networks (TF-GRNs; defined here as the set of 8

regulatory relationships between TFs and their putative target genes) represent an important mechanistic layer 9

linking genetic variation to disease. Meanwhile, the growing availability of large-scale transcriptomic resources, 10 

including bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing datasets spanning diverse tissues and biological conditions, 11 

provides unprecedented opportunities to identify cellular programs relevant to disease genetics. 12 

Several approaches have been developed to integrate either TF-GRNs or transcriptomic variation with 13 

GWAS signals. On the TF-GRN side, S-LDSC has been applied to TF ChIP-seq–derived annotations to 14 

partition heritability across regulatory elements 6 . IMPACT 7 integrates TF motif information with epigenomic 15 

features, including ChIP-seq and chromatin accessibility data, to define cell-state-specific regulatory 16 

annotations for heritability enrichment analysis. More recently, Perturb-multiome has enabled experimental 17 

reconstruction of TF-GRNs through combinatorial CRISPR perturbation screens coupled with joint single-cell 18 

transcriptomic and chromatin accessibility profiling 8 . However, these approaches primarily focus on defining 19

On the transcriptome side, the most widely used framework is LD score regression applied to 3

specifically expressed genes (LDSC-SEG) 9 , which tests for enrichment of causal variants near genes exhibiting 4

label-specific expression patterns. sc-linker 10 extends this framework to single-cell RNA sequencing 5

(scRNA-seq) data by deriving gene programs and linking them to SNP annotations through tissue-specific 6

enhancer–gene maps before applying S-LDSC. Although these approaches have successfully highlighted 7

disease-relevant cell types and cellular states, they rely primarily on transcriptomic variation and do not 8

explicitly model TF-centered regulatory programs. Consequently, they cannot directly assess whether disease 9

heritability is concentrated within transcriptional programs coordinated by specific TF regulatory networks. 10 

A second limitation of existing transcriptome-based approaches is their reliance on predefined cellular 11 

annotations. Methods such as LDSC-SEG and sc-linker typically require investigators to define cell types, 12 

cellular states, or comparison groups a priori, potentially overlooking regulatory programs that transcend 13 

conventional cell-type boundaries. Several efforts have sought to reduce reliance on predefined annotations. 14 

One such approach is scDRS 11 , which scores individual cells based on the aggregate expression of 15 

GWAS-prioritized gene sets derived using MAGMA 12 . While powerful for identifying disease-relevant cells, 16 

scDRS aggregates genetic associations at the gene level and therefore does not explicitly model SNP-level 17 

genetic architecture. Another recent approach leverages paired snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq profiling to define 18 

annotations based on chromatin accessibility patterns associated with transcriptomic variation, followed by 19

Together, these limitations highlight an unmet need for a framework that integrates TF-GRNs with 4

transcriptomic variation, operates without predefined cellular annotations, and directly leverages genome-wide 5

SNP-level genetic information. To address this challenge, we developed CATaN ( Canonical correlation Analysis 6

of Transcriptome and TF-gene regulatory Networks), an unsupervised framework that identifies shared variation 7

between transcriptomes and TF-GRNs through canonical correlation analysis (CCA) 14 , producing genome-wide, 8

single-base-resolution scores that reflect the regulatory potential underlying this shared variation. Although CCA 9

has previously been used to identify shared variance between upstream regulatory sequence motifs and gene 10 

expression profiles 15 , to our knowledge it has not been applied to integrate TF ChIP-seq-derived regulatory 11 

networks with transcriptomic data for heritability enrichment analysis. Moreover, unlike the sequence motifs 12 

used in that earlier work, the ChIP-seq-derived TF-GRNs employed by CATaN capture cell-type-specific TF 13 

occupancy, providing complementary regulatory information 16 . By integrating TF-GRN information with 14 

transcriptomic variation, CATaN identified disease-relevant regulatory programs and heritability enrichments 15 

that had not been captured by existing transcriptome-only approaches. 16

Results 1

Overview of CATaN 2

Briefly, CATaN consists of three steps (Figure 1A): 3

1. Construct a TF-GRN matrix (genes × TF ChIP–seq samples) using exponential weighted TF-gene 4

connectivity scores, with a half-decay distance of 10 kb selected to maximize how strongly CCA captures 5

shared variation between the transcriptome matrix and the TF-GRN matrix; this distance captures regulatory 6

contributions extending beyond proximal promoters, including enhancer-driven regulation (Methods; 7

Supplementary Note; Figures S1B and S1C). 8

2. Construct a transcriptome matrix (genes × samples) with gene-expression values. 9

3. Apply CCA to link the two matrices and transform the CCA loading into genome-wide, 10 

single-base-pair-resolution scores. 11 

We constructed a TF-GRN matrix by curating and processing a large TF ChIP-seq compendium 12 

comprising 2,868 profiles covering 410 TFs across 24 tissues and 287 cell types (Figure S1A and Table S1), 13 

and assembled transcriptome matrices spanning diverse biological contexts. CATaN applies CCA to identify 14 

shared variation between these two matrices. Applying CCA embeds transcriptome and TF ChIP-seq samples 15 

into a shared canonical space, yielding three outputs per canonical component: (i) TF sample loadings, (ii) 16 

transcriptome sample loadings, and (iii) gene scores in the transcriptome embedding (transcriptome gene 17 

projections) (Supplementary Note; Figure S1D). Intuitively, CATaN linearly projects transcriptomic variation onto 18 

TF-GRN components to yield genome-wide annotation tracks. TF sample loadings can be converted into 19

genome-wide annotation tracks using ChIP-seq binding profiles (CC annotation; Figure 1B and Methods), 1

constituting the fourth output of CATaN. Briefly, the CC scores of each SNP is calculated as the sum of TF 2

sample loadings of all TF peaks overlapping the SNP for the given CC. The top 10% and bottom 10% of SNPs 3

by score were each used to define a separate binary annotation. These CC annotations are used to test 4

heritability enrichment and to prioritize candidate loci for downstream experimental characterization. 5

Transcriptome sample loadings, together with heritability enrichment results, enable identification of the specific 6

samples and cell types driving heritable disease risk. The singular values (SVs) of CCA serve as an indicator of 7

model fit, reflecting how well each CC captures shared biological processes from the TF-GRN matrix and the 8

transcriptome matrix. 9

Directional consistency between TF and transcriptome matrices 11 

CATaN identifies gene expression signatures that exhibit high co-variance across TF-GRN and transcriptome 12 

matrices, based on the assumption that genes directly regulated by a given transcription factor are 13 

predominantly up- or down-regulated in a coherent manner. We tested this assumption using three independent 14 

lines of evidence. We first evaluated a TF overexpression experiment. We overexpressed the NF-κB subunit 15 

RELA (also known as TF65) using a CRISPRa 17 system and quantified gene expression by RNA-sequencing 16 

(RNA-seq) (see Figure 2A and Methods). Quality control analyses indicated that the CRISPRa perturbation was 17 

effective: CRISPRa increased RELA expression approximately 7.5-fold versus control (Figure 2B); Principal 18 

Component Analysis (PCA) showed clear separation of RELA-overexpressed and control samples along PC1 19

Proof-of-concept analyses 4

We next evaluated CATaN from three complementary perspectives: (i) whether CATaN can correctly nominate 5

the TFs driving transcriptome variation, (ii) whether CATaN is robust against overfitting, and (iii) whether CC 6

annotation tracks recapitulate known gene–variant links. Using the RELA overexpression data described above 7

(n = 6 each for sgRELA and sgControl), we constructed a transcriptome matrix comprising perturbed and 8

non-perturbed samples and applied CATaN. Across 2,868 TF sample loadings, NF-κB ChIP-seq samples (n = 9

65) exhibited markedly higher CC1 loadings than other TFs (Figure 3A, Table S4; Fisher’s one-tailed test, odds 10 

ratio = 48; Bonferroni-corrected p = 1.68 × 10 −32 ). Permutation of gene labels in the transcriptome data (Figure 11 

S3A) substantially reduced the Z-scored CC1 loadings of RELA (Figure 3B), indicating that the observed signal 12 

was not driven by technical inflation. These results indicate that CATaN captures shared variation between 13 

transcriptome and TF-GRN and can nominate the TFs driving transcriptome shifts. 14 

To assess whether CATaN is robust against overfitting, we partitioned GTEx samples into two halves 15 

and independently applied CATaN to each subset. TF sample loadings from the two subsets were strongly 16 

correlated (Figure S3B). Moreover, when transcriptome gene projections derived from one subset (training 17 

subset) were used to project the held-out subset onto the same embedding space (Figure 3C), samples from 18 

the same tissue formed distinct clusters at corresponding positions (Figure 3D). Together, these results indicate 19

Finally, we evaluated our CC annotation tracks against fine-mapped eQTLs from GTEx v10 23 , which 3

CATaN decomposes GTEx transcriptomic variation into biologically relevant axes 10 

Having established that CATaN produces robust and interpretable outputs, we next examined whether its 11 

outputs provide coherent biologically relevant axes. GTEx is well suited for this purpose because its 12 

transcriptomic diversity and tissue-defining TFs are extensively characterized, enabling direct assessment of 13 

whether canonical axes capture known biology. CATaN embeds transcriptome and TF ChIP-seq samples into a 14 

shared canonical space, enabling joint characterization of gene expression and its upstream TF regulators. 15 

Each CC axis can be characterized through three CATaN outputs: transcriptome sample loadings (Tr-loading), 16 

TF sample loadings (TF-loading), and pathway enrichment of gene projections. We assessed the association 17 

between sample loadings and metadata using Fisher's exact test with Bonferroni correction (see Methods). 18

CATaN application to multiple datasets 13 

To assess the applicability of CATaN to multiple datasets, we applied it across eight transcriptome datasets 14 

spanning distinct biological contexts (Table 1): (i) bulk inter-tissue and cell-type dataset—GTEx (53 human 15

and (iii) whether transcriptome sample loadings are associated with sample metadata. SVs in the observed data 3

were on average 3.5-fold higher than those from null data in GTEx (Figure 4D). Because CATaN is label-free 4

and does not use TF sample metadata (410 TFs across 24 tissues), assessing how much of the variance in TF 5

sample loadings is explained by this metadata provides an orthogonal validity check. Variance partitioning 6

confirmed that TF identity (Figure S3C) and tissue each explained significantly non-zero variance in TF sample 7

loadings (29% and 22%, respectively, in GTEx). Notably, compared with GTEx (across-tissue heterogeneity), 8

human immune cell dataset and ImmGen (intra-immune cell heterogeneity) showed smaller tissue components 9

and larger TF components (tissue 6.4% and TF 37% in human immune cell dataset, tissue 7.9% and TF 40% in 10 

ImmGen), indicating that CATaN metrics reflect the input data modality (Figure S3D, Table S6A). Reciprocally, 11 

transcriptome sample loadings also showed significant associations with metadata (Table S6B), further 12 

supporting the validity of CATaN. Together, these analyses show that CATaN recovers biologically meaningful 13 

covariation between TF-GRNs and diverse transcriptomes. 14

SNP-heritability enrichment across diverse transcriptome datasets 10 

We applied S-LDSC to the 740 CC annotation tracks (top and bottom deciles × 10 CCs × 37 datasets) derived 11 

from eight biologically diverse transcriptome datasets (Table 1). We quantified SNP-heritability enrichment 12 

across 90 traits/diseases. In total, 588 significant enrichments among 63,700 applications were detected, 13 

spanning 69 traits and diseases. To confirm cross-ancestry robustness of CC annotation tracks, we compared 14 

the adjusted   values derived from five bulk datasets between EUR and EAS GWAS for RA and found a high 15

Discussion 1

In this study, we developed CATaN, an unsupervised framework that integrates transcriptome data with 2

TF-mediated gene regulatory networks via CCA to identify genome-wide annotation tracks enriched for disease 3

heritability. CATaN differs from conventional approaches in three major ways. First, it is fully unsupervised, 4

enabling comprehensive exploration of transcriptomic variance without relying on predefined labels, an 5

advantage when axes of transcriptomic variation and heritability enrichment are not aligned. Second, beyond 6

CC annotation tracks, CATaN provides TF sample loadings, transcriptome sample loadings, and gene 7

projections, facilitating mechanistic interpretation in terms of specific TFs, sample metadata, and gene 8

programs. Third, CC annotation tracks are built from TF sample loadings combined with ChIP-seq binding 9

profiles, in contrast to proximity to gene bodies alone. Collectively, these features make CC tracks a powerful 10 

resource for quantifying heritability enrichment while simultaneously providing mechanistic insight and 11 

facilitating the prioritization of candidate causal variants. 12 

CATaN can identify the specific cell types and biological states enriched for disease heritability without 13 

relying on predefined labels. In the T cell and B cell subsets of the RA synovium dataset, heritability-enriched 14 

cell types were identified. These findings are consistent with a previous multi-omics study integrating 15 

scATAC-seq and scRNA-seq 13 , which reported heritability enrichment in overlapping T cell populations. Notably, 16 

CATaN recovered these T cell associations using scRNA-seq data alone, highlighting its broader applicability to 17 

datasets where chromatin accessibility data are unavailable. Furthermore, CATaN additionally detected 18 

enrichment in B cell populations, including ABCs , which are implicated in RA pathogenesis 39 , and GC-like B 19

cells associated with ectopic germinal center formation 40 , but had not been highlighted in heritability analyses 1

based on chromatin accessibility 13 . Moreover, while the previous multi-omics study did not detect heritability 2

enrichment in GZMK/B ⁺ memory CD8 ⁺ T cells and proposed that their expansion reflects a secondary response 3

to inflammation 13 , CATaN identified enrichment in this population. This difference may arise because CATaN 4

captures TF-GRN-based regulatory signal rather than chromatin accessibility, although the contribution of 5

GZMK/B ⁺ CD8 ⁺ T cells to RA heritability remains to be established. More broadly, methods to project disease 6

heritability enrichment onto single-cell transcriptomic landscapes in a label-free manner remain scarce. Unlike 7

scDRS, which integrates GWAS data at the gene level, CATaN operates at the SNP level, retaining genetic 8

information that is aggregated in gene-based approaches such as scDRS. CATaN addresses this gap in 9

label-free methodology by providing a systematic framework to localize heritability signals within cellular 10 

heterogeneity, and we anticipate that its application to diverse single-cell transcriptome datasets will yield 11 

further biological insights. 12 

Our study presents four primary limitations. First, the TF ChIP-seq dataset was assembled by broadly 13 

curating all available public data at the time of analysis; nevertheless, substantial biases exist in both antibody 14 

targets and sampled tissues. For example, CTCF accounts for approximately 10% of all TFs, and 15 

immune-related TFs such as RELA and STAT family members are heavily represented. Likewise, approximately 16 

one-third of samples originate from immune cells. These imbalances reflect the current constraints in the 17 

quantity of publicly available ChIP-seq resources. Expansion of ChIP-seq datasets, particularly across diverse 18 

TFs and tissue contexts, will help mitigate these biases and is expected to further improve method performance. 19

Second, potential inaccuracies exist in model specification, including the strategy used to link TF binding sites to 1

genes. At present, no experimental gold standard exists for validating TF-gene regulatory links in a 2

genome-wide manner, making this an important area for future methodological and experimental development. 3

Third, our experimental fine-mapping analysis was limited to a single locus where both the GWAS lead variant 4

and the CC SNP achieved sufficient editing efficiency for caQTL comparison. Low editing efficiency, particularly 5

at GWAS lead variants, was the primary bottleneck, possibly reflecting reduced chromatin accessibility at these 6

sites. Expanding the experimental validation to additional loci will be necessary to more robustly evaluate the 7

utility of CC annotation for causal variant prioritization. Fourth, our single-cell heritability enrichment analyses 8

were performed at the cell level, where cells from the same donor are not statistically independent. The 9

cell-type-level signals we report therefore require validation with donor-aware approaches in larger cohorts. 10 

CATaN is a versatile framework applicable across diverse transcriptomic datasets. By structuring 11 

transcriptomic variance through the lens of TF-GRN information, it identifies components associated with 12 

disease heritability and paves the way for prioritizing regulatory programs and candidate variants in basic and 13 

clinical research. 14

(A) Schematic of Canonical correlation Analysis of Transcriptome and TF-gene regulatory Networks (CATaN). 1

CATaN takes two input matrices—a transcriptome matrix (genes × samples) and a TF-Gene Regulatory network 2

(TF-GRN) matrix (genes × TF ChIP-seq samples)—and applies canonical correlation analysis (CCA) to yield 3

three outputs per canonical component (CC): transcriptome sample loadings, TF sample loadings and 4

transcriptome gene projections. Singular values (SVs) serve as an indicator of CCA model fitness. TF sample 5

loadings are converted into genome-wide CC annotation tracks using ChIP-seq binding profiles and combined 6

with genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary statistics for SNP-heritability enrichment analysis. 7

Heritability-enriched samples and cell are then identified by combining transcriptome sample loadings and 8

heritability enrichment results. 9

(B) Generation of CC annotation tracks. TF sample loadings are assigned to TF peak regions. For each SNP 10 

used in linkage disequilibrium (LD) score regression, scores from all TF peaks overlapping the SNP are 11 

summed, while SNPs outside any peak receive a score of 0. Binary annotations are then created by assigning a 12 

value of 1 to SNPs in the top or bottom 10% of the score distribution and 0 to all others. Created with 13
