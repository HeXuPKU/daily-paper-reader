# Abstract 15 

Background 16 

Complex traits are influenced by numerous variants, most of which have regulatory effects on 17 

gene expression that can be mediated by DNA methylation. Molecular QTL mapping is an 18 

approach that aims to dissect these effects. However, obtaining molecular phenotypes on a 19 

large scale is challenging, particularly in livestock species. In cattle, an epigenotyping array 20 

called EpiChip has recently been developed in the European RUMIGEN project. The EpiChip, 21 

which contains 43,317 CpG sites distributed all over the bovine genome, enables large -scale 22 

measurement of DNA methylation.  This study aims to characterize the genetic determinism 23 

of blood DNA methylation in cows by estimating heritability and mapping  cis - and  trans -24 

methylation QTLs (meQTLs). 25 

Results 26 

Whole blood  samples from  4,457  genotyped  Holstein cows w ere epigenotyped . Across all CpG 27 

sites, the heritability estimates averaged 24.6%. The local meQTL mapping at sequence -level 28 

for variable CpG sites (SD > 2.5%; n = 28,806) detected  cis -meQTLs for  80 .1% of the CpG sites, 29 

with sentinel SNPs located close to their associated CpGs. A two -step analysis was also 30 

conducted to identify long -range associations, with a particular focus on  trans -meQTL 31 

hotspots. First, we identified CpG -SNP  trans -associations using medium -density genotypes 32 

(50k SNPs) that revealed  31 ,846  SNPs with significant effects on 1 to  530  trans -CpG sites. Then, 33 

regions associated  with at least  34  independent  trans -CpGs were retained  defining 31 hotpots. 34 

For each hotspot, a local sequence -level GWAS was conducted using the first principal 35 

component derived from the associated  trans -CpGs.  Out of the  31 detected hotspots, t hree 36 

were located close to transcription factor genes ( RUNX1 , NFIC  and  FOXA3 ) for which the 37 

associated  trans -CpGs were enriched for the corresponding binding motif. Two other hotspots 38 

were located within  KDM5A  and  KDM5B , and their corresponding  trans -CpGs were strongly 39 

overrepresented in H3K4me3 narrow peaks in blood as well as in other tissues. 40 

Conclusions 41 

By identifying functional candidate genes associated with blood DNA methylation in cattle, 42 

these findings provide new insights into the regulatory architecture of DNA methylation in 43 

mammals, highlighting the value of large -scale molecular data from livestock populations. 44

# Introduction 45 

Cattle is a livestock species of major economic importance and a powerful model for studying 46 

the genetic architecture of complex traits in mammals. The availability of large phenotypic and 47 

genomics datasets, often comprising tens to hundreds of thousands of individuals, has 48 

enabled extensive genome -wide association analyses that have identified numerous 49 

quantitative trait loci (QTL) affecting a wide range of traits of interest. Although several causal 50 

variants have been mapped to coding regions, it is now widely recognized that a substantial 51 

proportion of the genetic variation underlying complex traits is mediated by regulatory 52 

variants that influence gene expression and other molecular processes  [1 –3] . However, 53 

identifying these regulatory variants and elucidating the molecular mechanisms through 54 

which they affect phenotypes remain challenging. To address this gap, large -scale initiatives 55 

have been undertaken to generate functional genomic resources in cattle  [4 –6] , with the aim 56 

of linking regulatory variation to intermediate molecular phenotypes and ultimately complex 57 

traits. Such resources facilitate the characterization of molecular QTL (molQTL) and provide 58 

novel insights into the regulatory architecture of the genome  [6 –8] .59 

Among the molQTLs, expression QTLs (eQTLs) and splicing QTLs (sQTLs) have been the most 60 

extensively studied in cattle, revealing the wide genetic control of transcriptional regulation 61 

[7,9] . In contrast, the genetic determinants of DNA methylation variation remain much less 62 

explored, despite the key role of DNA methylation as a major epigenetic mechanism involved 63 

in gene regulation, chromatin organization, and genome stability. Mapping methylation QTLs 64 

(meQTL) provides a powerful framework to investigate how genetic variants influence the 65 

epigenome and to uncover regulatory pathways linking genetic variation to phenotypic 66 

diversity. In humans, large -scale studies have demonstrated that meQTLs are abundant across 67 

the genome and have provided valuable insights into the genetic architecture of complex 68 

traits and diseases  [10 –14] . However, similar investigations in livestock species have been 69 

limited, largely due to the cost and technical challenges associated with measuring DNA 70 

methylation in large numbers of animals. Recently, the development of the RUMIGEN EpiChip 71 

has opened new opportunities for large -scale epigenomic studies in cattle. This chip targets 72 

43,317 CpG sites distributed across the genome and was designed on data from different 73 

studies comprising CpGs where methylation varies according to health status, physiological 74

In this study, we leveraged a cohort of 4,457 cows epigenotyped using the RUMIGEN EpiChip 77 

and genotyped with a medium -density BeadChip in order to investigate the genetic regulation 78 

of DNA methylation in blood. By combining meQTL mapping with the integration of functional 79 

genomic information, we identified several candidate regulators with genome -wide effects, 80 

highlighting different regulatory mechanisms that contribute to epigenetic variation in cattle. 81 

# Results 82 

We investigated the genetic regulation of blood DNA methylation by estimating the variance 83 

components of methylation rates and identifying meQTLs. The main steps are presented in 84 

Fig. 1. For each CpG site targeted by the EpiChip, heritability was estimated using a genomic 85 

relationship matrix. Methylation QTL mapping was performed for the probes that display 86 

inter -individual variations (SD>2.5%), corresponding to 28,806 CpG sites.  Cis -meQTL mapping 87 

was done at sequence level from imputed medium -density genotypes.  Trans -meQTL mapping 88 

was done at the medium density. Genomic regions associated with numerous  trans -CpGs 89 

were then re -assessed at the sequence level. In this second analysis, the coordinates of the 90 

cows along the first principal component of the  trans -associated CpGs were used as a 91 

summarizing variable (see “Methods” section for further details). 92

Heritability of DNA methylation at the CpG sites targeted by the EpiChip 104 

Among the 40,676 CpG sites analyzed, the average heritability was 24.6% (SD = 22.5%), with 105 

64.5% of CpGs showing a heritability  greater than 10%, indicating that a substantial proportion 106 

of DNA methylation variation is under genetic control. However, heritability estimates were 107 

highly variables across CpG sites, ranging from zero to nearly one (Fig. 2a). CpGs annotated 108 

within promoter regions displayed low heritability estimates while those located in enhancer 109 

regions tended to have higher heritability estimates (Fig. 2c). In contrast, differences in 110 

heritability distributions across the CpG island landscape were less pronounced (Fig. 2b). 111

# Discussion 284 

Leveraging data from 4,457 Holstein cows, this study represents, to our knowledge, the largest 285 

investigation of the genetic determinism of DNA methylation conducted in cattle and, more 286 

broadly, in livestock species. Overall, DNA methylation heritability estimates were moderate 287 

and most genetic effects on methylation operated over short genomic distances. In addition, 288 

our two -step strategy for mapping  trans -meQTL hotspots enabled the precise identification of 289 

positional candidate genes, while the integration of functional genomic annotations provided 290 

strong support for several hotspot regions and their potential regulatory mechanisms. 291 

To date, only one study has estimated the heritability of DNA methylation in cattle. Conducted 292 

in Holstein bull sperm using reduced representation bisulfite sequencing and restricted to 293 

variable CpG sites (SD > 5%), that study reported an average heritability estimate of 0.26  [31] ,294 

which is comparable to our findings. Notably, these estimates are higher than those reported 295 

in humans, where blood DNA methylation heritability estimates typically range from ~0.10 to 296 

0.20  [11,12,14] . Such differences may partly reflect disparities in the genomic representation 297 

and design of epigenotyping arrays. Consistent with previous human studies, we observed 298 

lower heritability in promoter associated CpGs and higher heritability in enhancer -associated 299 

CpGs regions, potentially reflecting differences in DNA methylation levels or regulatory 300 

dynamics between these genomic regions. 301 

Gunasekara et al.  [32]  previously identified correlated regions of systemic interindividual 302 

variation (CoRSIVs) in humans and demonstrated that these regions are under strong genetic 303 

control. The existence of CoRSIVs has also been reported in cattle and shown to be associated 304 

with genetic variation  [16] , in agreement with our results showing higher heritability estimates 305 

for CpGs located within CorSIVs. We also identified a substantially higher proportion of CpGs 306 

associated with  cis -meQTLs ( 80 .1% of variable CpGs) than has been reported in human whole 307

genetic variants. As previously mentioned, methodological differences between studies, 309 

including differences in CpG selection criteria and epigenotyping arrays, may contribute, at 310 

least in part, to these discrepancies. 311 

A major finding of this study is the identification of several  trans -meQTL hotspots associated 312 

with chromatin regulation and transcriptional processes. Previous studies have shown that 313 

trans  effects on DNA methylation can arise through transcription factors and variations in their 314 

binding sites  [12,14,18] . We observed similar patterns for several hotspots, including  H22 315 

involving  FOXA3 , a TF previously identified as a  trans -meQTL hotspot in human blood  [18]  and 316 

RUNX1  (hotspot H2) which has been implicated in  trans -regulatory methylation networks  [18] .317 

Histone modifications constitute another plausible mechanism associating genetic variants to 318 

CpG methylation levels  [33] . In rats, Rintisch et al.  [34]  demonstrated that histone QTLs can 319 

influence gene expression. Our results further suggest that histone modifiers may also affect 320 

CpG methylation at multiple distal genomic regions, as illustrated by hotspots H 7 and H1 9. In 321 

addition, several hotspots mapped to regions containing genes playing a key role in DNA 322 

methylation or genes known to interact with them ( CDCA7  (H3),  TET2  (H8),  UHRF1  (H9), 323 

DNMT3A  (H15),  DNMT3B  (H16),  BTA -MIR142  (H24),  TET1  (H30) ). Among these,  BTA -MIR142 ,324 

a microRNA -encoding gene, emerged as a compelling candidate underlying  trans -meQTL 325 

hotspot H2 4. BTA -MIR142  is involved in hematopoietic and immune cell regulatory pathways 326 

and has been shown to target  TET2  [35] , a key enzyme mediating active DNA demethylation. 327 

We therefore hypothesize that the  trans -methylation effects associated with H2 4 may arise 328 

from a  cis -regulatory variant affecting  MIR142  expression, which could in turn modulate  TET2 329 

activity in  trans , thereby influencing methylation levels at numerous CpGs distributed across 330 

the genome. Notably, the lead SNP (Chr19:9302775) co -localizes with a CTCF -binding site 331 

identified in blood using CUT&RUN data ( Additional file 1: Table S4) . Given the established 332 

role of CTCF in shaping chromatin architecture and regulating gene expression, local 333 

chromatin organization may influence  MIR142  expression. However, we cannot exclude the 334 

possibility that this hotspot partly reflects interindividual differences in blood cell composition 335 

rather than a direct regulatory effect on DNA methylation. More generally, although 336 

integrative analyses enabled the prioritization of candidate genes and biological mechanisms, 337 

experimental validation will be required to confirm these hypotheses. 338

When molecular QTLs are analyzed in bulk tissues, particularly  trans -hotspots, detected 339 

associations may reflect loci influencing cellular composition rather than direct molecular 340 

regulation  [3] . Although we adjusted for three major cell types, this correction remains 341 

incomplete given the numerous blood cell populations present in whole blood. Therefore, 342 

genes involved in hematopoiesis or immune -cell abundance represent plausible candidates 343 

underlying meQTL hotspots detected in blood. Although evidence in cattle remains limited, 344 

many of the positional candidate genes identified in this study have previously been 345 

associated with hematological traits in humans  [17] . These hotspots therefore represent 346 

promising candidates for the genetic regulation of blood -related phenotypes in cattle, 347 

although additional studies will be necessary to validate these associations and clarify the 348 

underlying biological mechanisms. 349 

Limitations 350 

This study has several limitations. First, the two -step approach used to identify long -range 351 

associations focused on  trans -meQTL hotspots, therefore masking more specific long -range 352 

SNP -CpG regulatory relationships. In addition, our colocalization analyses were restricted to 353 

trans -meQTL hotspots and selected phenotypic traits of interest. The absence of 354 

transcriptomic data for the studied cohort also precluded the identification of CpG sites 355 

associated with variations in gene expression and limited our ability to investigate causal 356 

regulatory cascades linking genetic variation, DNA methylation, and gene expression. 357 

Although public resources such as cattleGTEx are available, these datasets are heterogeneous 358 

and meta -analytic in nature and do not provide matched methylation and transcriptomic 359 

measurements from the same blood samples, thereby limiting their integration with our 360 

analyses. 361 

# Conclusions 362 

In summary, this study provides the first large -scale integrative characterization of the genetic 363 

architecture underlying blood DNA methylation in a livestock species, building on recently 364 

developed functional annotation resources in cattle. Our findings demonstrate that DNA 365 

methylation is substantially influenced by genetic variation, with regulatory mechanisms that 366 

largely mirror those observed in humans, including strong  cis  regulation and the presence of 367 

biologically meaningful  trans -meQTL hotspots. These results contribute to a better 368
