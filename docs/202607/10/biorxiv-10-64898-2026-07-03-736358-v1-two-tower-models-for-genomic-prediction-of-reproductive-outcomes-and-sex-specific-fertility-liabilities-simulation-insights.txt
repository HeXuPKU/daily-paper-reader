Abstract 9

Many biological characteristics arise by interactions between more than one biological 10 

organism or unit. Fertilization success in sexually reproducing species represents such an 11 

extended phenotype where both mates are required to be fertile for a successful outcome. 12 

Consequently, predictive models should account for the joint nature of reproductive 13 

performance while offering interpretable estimates for individual mate contributions. Recent 14 

advances in genomics and machine learning (ML) provide standardized, high-dimensional 15 

genetic information on one hand and computational tools capable of modeling complex 16 

biological systems on the other. Here, we construct and evaluate two-tower (TT) machine 17 

learning architectures for genomic prediction of binary reproductive outcomes and recovery of 18 

sex-specific fertility liabilities. Simulated datasets, generated under a range of genetic 19 

architectures, were utilized to compare multilayer perceptron (TT-MLP), convolutional neural 20 

network (TT-CNN), and L1-regularized linear (TT-LASSO) two-tower models. Simulation 21 

scenarios varied sex-specific heritabilities, genetic correlations, infertility prevalence, mating 22 

structure, and sex-specific infertility rates. Models were evaluated with regard to their ability 23 

to predict reproductive success at pair level and also recover true underlying genetic values for 24 

male and female fertility. Prediction accuracy increased with the underlying heritable 25 

component as expected, while sex-specific tower-scores successfully recovered latent fertility 26 

liabilities despite models being trained only on observed joint outcomes. TT-LASSO achieved 27 

the highest overall classification performance, whereas TT-MLP provided more balanced and 28 

consistent recovery of sex-specific genetic values across scenarios. An additional simulation, 29 

incorporating genotype-dependent mate compatibility demonstrated advantages of fully-30 

connected neural networks for capturing non-additive interactions. These results indicate that 31 

two-tower frameworks provide a powerful approach for modeling reproductive traits, enabling 32

Introduction 37 

Reproductive efficiency is of great importance in both terrestrial and aquatic animal 38 

agriculture. The reliable generation of new production and breeding stock directly affects both 39 

sustainability and profitability (Cabrera 2014; Gjedrem and Robinson 2014; Olsen et al. 2023; 40 

Wang et al. 2024; Oberpenning et al. 2026). Moreover, fertility is a key trait for genetic 41 

improvement efforts, as selection is only meaningful if superior animals can successfully 42 

reproduce and pass on their enhanced genetic merit to subsequent generations. Consequently, 43 

extensive efforts have been traditionally directed toward fertility monitoring and improvement, 44 

including proxy traits (e.g., sperm quality, estrus detection, hormonal profiles) (Waberski et al. 45 

2022; Mikkola et al. 2024), as well as biotechnologically assisted induction of gametogenesis 46 

and reproduction (Mylonas et al. 2003; Ferré et al. 2020; Lonergan 2024). 47 

However, fertilization success is not an individual trait. Instead, it is by definition expressed at 48 

levels beyond the individual, involving at least two parties. Such general aggregate biological 49 

outcomes are in principle joint phenotypes that require two or more individuals for their 50 

expression and are often called extended phenotypes (Dawkins 1982; Queller 2014). 51 

Considering fertilization success in sexually reproducing organisms, the unit of expression is 52 

the mating pair. In this context, male and female fertility can be seen as latent propensities, 53 

probabilities of being capable of successful reproduction. The precise interpretation of these 54 

latent phenotypes depends on how the joint trait is defined (e.g. binary success/failure, repeated 55 

measures, time to pregnancy). Nevertheless, irrespective of the specific definition, fertilization 56 

success cannot generally be expressed as a linear combination of male and female liabilities. 57 

Instead, the biological mechanism is inherently non-linear since successful fertilization 58 

requires both individuals to be fertile. A mathematical representation of this dependency could 59 

be a multiplicative system in which the product of sex-specific propensities yields the 60 

probability of fertilization success at the pair level (David et al. 2009; David et al. 2011; Pappas, 61 

Johnsson, et al. 2025). 62

Under such assumption, Bayesian hierarchical models and two-tower machine learning (ML) 63 

models have been previously described and tested on empirical records from aquaculture 64 

breeding programs (Pappas, Johnsson, et al. 2025; Pappas, Debes, et al. 2025). Two-tower 65 

models are ML architectures using two separate sub-models to map two distinct, high-66 

dimensional inputs into shared metrics, often distance or similarity (Covington et al. 2016; Liao 67 

et al. 2025; Liu et al. 2025). The implementation for fertilization prediction includes 68 

modification of the general two-tower setup to encode single, interpretable tower scores instead 69 

of low-dimensional vector representations (embeddings). The latter allows for analytical 70 

machinery, where the model is trained on aggregate response but additionally predicts sex-71 

specific liabilities that can be interpreted as breeding values in case of genetic input (SNP 72 

genotypes). 73 

Over the past decades, genomics and other high-throughput technologies have revolutionized 74 

agriculture. Hybridization-based arrays and automated analytical pipelines have enabled cost-75 

efficient, large-scale SNP genotyping, substantially accelerating selective breeding and 76 

genomic evaluation programs (Georges et al. 2019; Misztal et al. 2020). Since these 77 

technologies generate high-dimensional and increasingly complex datasets, robust analytical 78 

frameworks become essential for capturing genetic variance and optimizing prediction 79 

strategies (Meuwissen et al. 2015; Zhao et al. 2022; Zhao and Cheng 2023). 80 

In this context, simulation is a powerful tool for evaluating model performance under diverse 81 

genetic architectures when the true underlying structure is unknown or inaccessible (Bančič et 82 

al. 2025). The ability to control demographic history, variance components and covariance 83 

structure, allows detailed evaluation of model behavior under predefined conditions. In this 84 

study, we employed a series of simulations to investigate the properties and classification 85 

behavior of TT-models under varying scenarios. Specifically, we evaluated three ML 86 

architectures for predicting aggregate reproductive outcomes and recovering sex-specific 87 

fertility liabilities. By manipulating male and female genetic contributions independently, as 88 

well as correlation patterns and sex-specific structure, we assessed how predictive accuracy 89 

varies with underlying variance components and data structure. 90

Methods 93 

Simulations 94 

A set of scenarios was simulated using R/AlphaSimR v2.1.0 (Gaynor et al. 2021) with five 95 

replicates per scenario. All simulated datasets originated from a common founding population, 96 

generated with the runMacs wrapper function with 4,000 founder individuals, the “GENERIC” 97 

population history which represents a gradually shrinking effective population size similar to 98 

those of domestic animals, and a genome consisting of 10 chromosomes. Genetic architectures 99 

were simulated using 100 QTLs in total and genotypes were recorded using a simulated SNP 100 

chip (5,000 markers). 101 

For each scenario/replicate, simulation parameters were reinitialized every time before five 102 

generations of random selection and mating. Each generation consisted of 2,000 crosses, with 103 

15 progeny per cross resulting in a final population size of 30,000 individuals. In the final 104 

generation, 15,000 pseudo-crosses were generated per replicate for testing. More specifically, 105 

breeding pair allocations in this generation were based on binary phenotypes to achieve the 106 

target infertility prevalence. Breeders were ranked and the lowest ranking animals were 107 

classified as infertile according to a threshold corresponding to the specified prevalence. Thus, 108 

in scenarios with balanced sex-specific infertility, the proportion of infertile individuals within 109 

each sex was set to the square root of the pair-level prevalence. 110

Fourteen different scenarios were created to evaluate the effect of heritability, inter-sexual 113

In scenarios evaluating different sex-specific heritabilities ( HER scenario type), all pairwise 117

resulting in six unique scenarios (HER_1–HER_6). These scenarios covered cases with equal 119

Two-tower models 142 

Given sex-limited input variables, two-tower modeling frameworks can be employed to predict 143 

the observable response at the pair level (Pappas, Debes, et al. 2025). In such frameworks, sex-144 

specific predictors influence latent propensities within each sex that in turn fuse 145 

multiplicatively. This approach mirrors a biologically realistic structure for reproduction and 146 

maintains interpretability of sex-specific contributions. 147

submodels and utilize their predictions downstream. More specifically, given vectors of omic 149

Model training 239 

For each simulated dataset, one external 80% - 20% split took place with crosses indexed for 240 

either training or evaluation. For each replicate, the same stratified partitioning was kept 241 

constant for all three models for improved comparability. Stratification ensured avoidance of 242 

sire and/or dam leakage, meaning that pairs sharing common breeders were not allowed to 243 

appear both in training and testing sets which would otherwise lead to overestimation of 244 

performance. All models were trained for a maximum of 100 epochs using the tuned 245 

hyperparameters ( Table S1 ) and the Adam (Adaptive Moment Estimator) optimizer (Kingma 246 

and Ba 2017). A small subset (12.5%) of the training data was utilized as an internal validation 247 

to guide early stopping after 7 epochs with no improvement of the area under the curve of the 248 

receiver operator characteristic (ROC-AUC). Stopping was followed by restoration of the best 249 

model. 250 

251 

Model evaluation 252 

The evaluation set was used to test the binary classifier (threshold at 0.5 for P) using the ROC-253 

AUC metric but also the recovery of true genetic value (TGV) of the animals in the sex-specific 254 

tower outputs ( m and f in Figure 1A ) via Pearson’s correlation coefficients. Models were 255 

compared against each other, and their properties were explored by addressing across-scenario 256 

performance and with base scenarios as benchmarks. 257

Altering the relative prevalence of male and female infertility had clearer effects. Prediction 315 

accuracy decreased when infertility was more common in the sex with the less heritable fertility 316 

(simulated as male, SEX_1) and increased when it was more common in the opposite sex, 317 

where almost half the variance of the latent phenotype is additive genetic (simulated as female, 318 

SEX_2). Interestingly, TGV recovery for the latter scenarios revealed an information 319 

compensation mechanism when males were mostly responsible for pair-infertility. In this case 320 

(SEX_1), correlations of male TGVs with the respective liabilities were notably uplifted 321 

compared to the BASE_2 scenario, while on the other end, models relied heavier on the female 322 

factor when dam infertility was more prevalent (SEX_2). 323 

Finally, as the introduction of pairwise mate compatibility effects was accompanied by an 324 

increase in prevalence (mean = 0.54), there was an overall lift in predictive performance across 325 

all evaluated models ( Figure 4 ). To assess whether architectures that jointly model sire and 326 

dam genotype vectors (after concatenation) could better capture such effects, a fully-connected 327 

MLP was additionally evaluated alongside the TT-MLP and TT-LASSO models which 328 

previously performed well in scenarios without compatibility effects. While the MLP exhibited 329 

the lowest performance under the baseline scenario, it showed the largest performance gain 330 

following the extensive introduction of compatibility effects and achieved the highest 331

Discussion 343 

Recently, ML models have entered the field of genomic prediction in agricultural systems and 344 

have been proposed as powerful and flexible alternatives to conventional parametric 345 

approaches (Ogutu et al. 2011; Montesinos-López et al. 2021; Palaiokostas 2021; Zhao et al. 346

It is The copyright holder for this preprint this version posted July 9, 2026. ;https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.03.736358 doi: bioRxiv preprint

2022; Zhao and Cheng 2023; Lourenço et al. 2024; Alemu et al. 2025). Simultaneously, 347 

complex two-tower architectures are becoming prominent in life sciences revolutionizing 348 

prediction of biological structure and interactions (Bouatta et al. 2021; Liao et al. 2025; Liu et 349 

al. 2025). In this study, we performed simulations and demonstrated a use-case of simple, 350 

tailored TT-models for the prediction of reproductive success and sex-specific fertility 351 

liabilities given genetic input. 352 

The tested TT-models demonstrated sufficient capacity to predict binary reproductive success 353 

outcomes with accuracy scaling proportionally to the heritabilities of the underlying latent-354 

fertility phenotypes. Interestingly, the magnitude of this relationship is in line with results from 355 

empirical studies utilizing pedigree-derived input features (Pappas, Debes, et al. 2025). Across 356 

scenarios, TT-LASSO tended to show superior performance across replicates for binary 357 

classification, consistent with stronger regularization machinery and lower model complexity 358 

(fewer learnable parameters). This suggests that controlling data to model-parameters 359 

cardinality ratios may be more important than using highly flexible architectures incorporating 360 

neural networks in this setting. Of course, the latter class of models is in reality highly 361 

parametrizable and optimization is still in question since a relatively constrained searchable 362 

space was explored only for the baseline scenario. Thus, effective hyperparameters should not 363 

be assumed known, especially for the simulated datasets not corresponding to the 364 

aforementioned benchmark. 365 

Under scenarios incorporating intra-sexual genetic correlations (COR scenarios ), no 366 

noteworthy performance gain was observed compared to the base condition. This is consistent 367 

with the model architecture that processes sire and dam features in separate towers and only 368 

combines them multiplicatively, thus lacking a mechanism to capture across-sex covariance or 369 

interaction effects. The latter is of course a consequence of the design of our architectures that 370 

prioritized interpretable tower-scores on top of pure accuracy for joint outcomes. That strategy 371 

offers additional value for selective breeding since it provides a means of ranking breeders and 372 

thus better-informed selection aiming to shift the population mean. 373 

Overall, performance of the binary success/failure classifiers differed notably from 374 

benchmarks only under two scenario-types. First, pair-level prevalence of infertility where 375 

denser cases (PREV_2) seemed to assist model learning, especially in the case of LASSO 376 

probably due to decreased model complexity. Second, asymmetrical sex-specific infertility 377 

rates had a profound effect on the prediction accuracy. That was likely due to the same reason 378

phenotypes, revealing sensitivity to the stronger signal-to-noise ratio when a larger or smaller 380 

proportion of fertilization failures originates from the sex with more heritable fertility. 381 

382 

As evident by the recovery of true genetic merit for mates, our TT models were able to 383 

disentangle sex-specific contributions despite being trained solely on joint responses. TT-MLP 384 

liabilities demonstrated adequate, moderate-high correlations with TGVs combined with 385 

consistent and reliable behaviour. On the other hand, TT-LASSO returned higher maxima, but 386 

recovery was collapsing for the sex representing low-heritable fertility. Interestingly, both the 387 

MATE and SEX scenarios suggested that this behavior was influenced not only by heritability, 388 

but also by how information was distributed between the sexes. In the MATE scenarios, TT-389 

LASSO showed improved recovery of male TGVs when dams outnumbered sires (MATE_1) 390 

and reduced recovery when the opposite mating structure was tested (MATE_2). Similarly, 391 

under SEX_1, where infertility was more prevalent in males, recovery of male TGVs improved 392 

relative to BASE_2 despite the lower male heritability. In contrast, under SEX_2, models relied 393 

more heavily on the female component, leading to stronger female TGV recovery and weaker 394 

recovery of male genetic effects. Compiled, these results suggest that increasing the amount of 395 

information associated with one sex can partially compensate for lower heritability, although 396 

the stronger genetic signal remained superiorly captured across scenarios. 397 

This finding can be explained by the intuition of the model which uses a common penalty 398 

hyperparameter and thus prioritizes strong regularization and learning the features with higher 399 

signal-to-noise (stronger heritable component). Consequently, TT-LASSO captures the more 400 

heritable component when information is limited, but can recover weaker genetic effects when 401 

the data structure provides additional observations to inform them. Practically, this implies that 402 

tailored, case-specific optimization of the 𝜆 penalty may be necessary to improve prediction of 403 

genetic merit, whereas TT-MLP can operate more predictably in genetic-parameter agnostic 404 

scenarios, with hyperparameters tuned under broader conditions generalizing well across 405 

different cases. 406 

Considering mate-compatibility as a function of male and female genotype interactions in a 407 

non-additive fashion revealed that fully-connected architectures might be advantageous for 408 

capturing components of variation that are not fully explained by independent fertility 409 

determinants from mates ( Figure 4 ). That said, TT-models also demonstrated a lift in absolute 410

relative marginal shadows of the pair-genotype interaction landscape ( Table 2 ), that partially 412 

enable the recovery of compatibility effects through tower-scores. Nevertheless, the higher 413 

prevalence of reproductive failure in the compatibility scenario and the corresponding shift to 414 

a more balanced case/control ratio may have also played a minor role in performance gains 415 

across all models. 416 

In practice, the proposed models are highly customizable and can possibly satisfy use-cases of 417 

extended phenotypes beyond reproduction. For example latent variables in simplified 418 

infectious-susceptible (Lipschutz-Powell et al. 2012; Pooley et al. 2025) systems could be 419 

analyzed with such methodology. For large scale implementation in genetic evaluations, 420 

encoder-towers could be helpful for pair-predictions. Pretrained, low-dimensional 421 

representations from towers could be stored in vector databases and retrieved for pair 422 

predictions on-the-go in a computationally efficient way (Xiong et al. 2025). The latter 423 

approach could also support multi-task prediction and downstream entangled networks that 424 

additionally capture mate compatibility effects (Martin et al. 2022). However, beyond the 425 

technical challenges associated with developing and validating such systems, producing 426 

interpretable individual liabilities may be difficult. One possible workaround would be to 427 

obtain proxy predictions by pairing a candidate genotype with the average genotype of a set of 428 

verified fertile mates, thereby approximating expected performance under a favorable mating 429 

context. Finally, partially connected networks could be incorporated within each tower to 430 

accommodate additional variables and modalities, enabling decomposition of genetic and 431 

environmental contributions. 432
