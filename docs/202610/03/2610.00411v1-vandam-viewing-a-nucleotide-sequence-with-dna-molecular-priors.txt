## ABSTRACT 

Contemporary Genomic Foundation Models (GFMs) rely on a DNA-as-a-string paradigm that employs masked token prediction objectives for pretraining. However, this abstraction does not explicitly model the biochemical, structural, and physical properties essential to biological function. Many molecular properties can be estimated from sequence using established biophysical models, so their utility lies not in providing an independent modality, but in introducing priors that training objectives can explicitly exploit. We introduce VANDAM, a framework that extends the training of GFMs with DNA molecular priors. In self-supervised training, VANDAM predicts regional molecular properties from pooled representations. When functional labels are available and can reward retaining molecular priors, local features are additionally injected at the input. VANDAM consistently improves downstream performance across four architecture families and nine held-out genomic tasks by complementing token-based objectives. Probing experiments further demonstrate that the use of molecular priors generalizes to other unseen molecular properties. 

## 1 Introduction 

Genomic foundation models (GFMs) learn transferable representations from large unlabeled genomic corpora and support tasks ranging from regulatory element annotation to variant effect prediction (Benegas et al., 2025c; Zhou et al., 2024; Boshar et al., 2025; Schiff et al., 2024; Brixi et al., 2026). Despite substantial diversity in architecture, scale, and context length, their training objectives remain largely inherited from natural language processing (NLP), where masked language modeling (MLM) and next-token prediction (NTP) train models to recover or predict token identity, treating nucleotides primarily as discrete symbols. In NLP, pre-training and many downstream applications share a common linguistic interface, as the model conditions on text to produce text. For generative tasks such as question answering, NTP directly trains the mechanism used to generate an answer. Genomic downstream tasks, by contrast, predict functional and phenotypic properties, such as regulatory activity, gene expression, and pathogenicity. Token prediction remains a useful general-purpose prior, but it does not directly require the representation to preserve the molecular properties relevant to these tasks. DNA differs from natural language in an important respect: whereas linguistic semantics is inferred from usage, many molecular properties of DNA have been characterized and can be computed directly from sequence. For example, nearest-neighbor models provide duplex free-energy contributions (SantaLucia Jr, 1998), and helical shape parameters can be estimated using models calibrated against all-atom simulations (Zhou et al., 2013; Chiu et al., 2016; Li et al., 2024). We therefore argue that GFMs should represent DNA not merely as a string of symbols, but also as a molecule with predictable structural, energetic, and chemical properties. arXiv:2610.00411v1 [cs.LG] 30 Sep 2026 VANDAM: viewing a nucleotide sequence with DNA molecular priors Motivated by this distinction and by the successful use of structural and functional priors in protein models, we introduce VANDAM, a framework that complements the conventional DNA-as-a-string view with a DNA-as-a-molecule view. VANDAM incorporates molecular priors through two complementary mechanisms. Local Feature Injection (LFI) augments token embeddings with local molecular descriptors, while Region Property Prediction (RPP) adds an auxiliary objective that asks pooled representations to predict sequence-derived molecular properties of genomic regions. RPP is employed in self-supervised pre-training, while LFI is additionally used in multi-task training, where functional labels can reward the use of molecular descriptors. Together, these mechanisms provide input-level and representation-level routes for grounding GFMs in the physical properties of DNA. Across four backbone families and nine held-out tasks, VANDAM improves the point estimate in 29 of 36 compute-matched pre-training comparisons and 33 of 36 multi-task comparisons; 25 and 23 comparisons, respectively, show statistical significance. Matched non-biological controls show that these gains depend on biological target content rather than auxiliary capacity alone, and RPP increases linear access to six of eight held-out qDNA descriptors on an independent confirmation split. We make four contributions. First, we frame genomic representation learning around objective-appropriate molecular grounding, treating DNA as a molecule. Second, we introduce LFI and RPP, which respectively inject local descriptors when functional labels can reward their use and directly supervise regional molecular properties during self-supervised training. Third, we evaluate VANDAM through compute-matched experiments across four backbone families and nine held-out tasks, and use matched non-biological controls to distinguish biological content from auxiliary capacity and optimization. Finally, we show with frozen-representation probes that RPP increases access to an independent family of held-out molecular descriptors. 

## 2 Related Work 

Genomic foundation models. Genomic foundation models (GFMs) follow the paradigm established by large language models: pre-train a sequence model on unlabeled genomic corpora, then transfer it to downstream functional genomics tasks. The landscape comprises diverse architectures and tokenization schemes for genomic sequence modeling. DNABERT-2 (Zhou et al., 2024) keeps a flat transformer encoder but replaces the overlapping k-mer tokenization of its predecessor (Ji et al., 2021) with byte-pair encoding; NTv3 (Boshar et al., 2025) adopts a U-Net-style convolutional– transformer hybrid; Caduceus (Schiff et al., 2024) replaces attention with a bi-directional Mamba block and, in its parameter-sharing variant, enforces reverse-complement equivariance architecturally; and Evo2 (Brixi et al., 2026) scales a StripedHyena2 hybrid to 40B parameters trained on 9.3 trillion tokens. Despite this diversity, most prominent GFMs rely primarily on pre-training objectives inherited from natural language processing: masked recovery or autoregressive prediction of token identity. Whether a token denotes a single nucleotide or a byte-pair-encoded sequence fragment, the objective treats it as a discrete symbol and does not explicitly supervise the physical properties of the underlying DNA sequence. This token-identity objective has recently been questioned, on the grounds that it captures local genomic syntax and motif structure while leaving broader functional context underdetermined. Several recent approaches therefore modify the training objective or the genomic representation: JEPA-DNA (Larey et al., 2026b) adds supervision of high-level functional embeddings of masked segments alongside token-level reconstruction; D3LM (Yang et al., 2026) reframes DNA modeling as discrete diffusion; UKBioBERT (Liu et al., 2026) continues masked-language modeling on sequences containing population-level genetic variation rather than reference sequence alone; and BioToken/BioFM (Medvedev et al., 2025) encodes variants and genomic annotations as biologically informed tokens. DNAGPT (Zhang et al., 2023) augments autoregressive pre-training with sequence-order classification and GC-content regression over sampled sequence regions. MutBERT (Long et al., 2025) represents each position as a population allele-frequency distribution; GPN-MSA (Benegas et al., 2025a) uses multi-species alignments as input, and PhyloGPN (Albors et al., 2025) incorporates phylogenetic alignments through its training objective while retaining single-sequence inference. These results suggest that the training signal and the representation of genomic context, not only capacity or architecture, affect the quality of learned representations. 

Biophysical supervision in representation learning. A relevant precedent is UTR-LM (Chu et al., 2024), which augments masked-nucleotide modeling of 5 ′ untranslated regions with auxiliary targets computed from the same sequence by ViennaRNA: secondary structure at masked positions and sequence-level minimum free energy. These targets provide biophysical supervision without introducing an additional experimental or structural modality. In DNA modeling, MIXBend (Yang et al., 2024) aligns DNABERT sequence representations with physicochemical features during self-supervised pre-training, but develops this approach specifically for DNA bendability prediction. Biophysical features in supervised prediction. Biophysical features carry well-established predictive signal for DNA, but much of the existing evidence comes from task-specific supervised models. DNA-shape features such as minor-groove width, computed from pentamer lookup tables built from all-atom Monte Carlo simulations (Zhou et al., 2013; Chiu et al., 2016; Li et al., 2017), improve transcription-factor-binding-site prediction over position-weight matrices and sequence-only baselines (Zhou et al., 2015; Mathelier et al., 2016). More recent work replaces the lookup tables with learned predictors that account for extended flanking context (Li et al., 2024). DNABERT-CoreProm-MM (Papineni et al., 2025) augments DNABERT embeddings with nucleotide composition, CpG density, minor-groove width, electrostatic potential, and other DNA-intrinsic features for supervised core-promoter prediction. Together, these studies establish the utility of sequence-derived molecular descriptors, but primarily within task-specific supervised settings. 

## 3 Methods 

3.1 From DNA as a String to DNA as a Molecule 

VANDAM complements the conventional DNA-as-a-string training paradigm with DNA molecular priors (Figure 1). These priors provide a biophysical inductive bias without requiring an additional experimental modality. In the following section, we first define the biological features used by VANDAM, then present Local Feature Injection and Region Property Prediction, and finally describe how these mechanisms are combined under different supervision regimes. 3VANDAM: viewing a nucleotide sequence with DNA molecular priors 

3.2 DNA Molecular Priors 

We define a mapping from each nucleotide in a DNA sequence to a set of molecular properties. We use the 13 structural features available in DNAshapeR (Chiu et al., 2016), whose original four-feature framework was introduced by Zhou et al. (2013) and expanded to 13 features by Li et al. (2017), plus minor-groove electrostatic potential (EP) from Chiu et al. (2017), for a total of 14 sequence-derived profiles. The features are organized into three groups: 

• Minor-groove environment: Minor-groove width (MGW) and electrostatic potential (EP). 

• Intra-base-pair geometry: Propeller twist (ProT), stretch, shear, buckle, opening, and stagger. 

• Inter-base-pair geometry: Helical twist (HelT), roll, tilt, rise, shift, and slide. All local properties are deterministic, model-derived molecular priors rather than direct measurements and do not capture cell-specific context, environmental conditions, or conformational variability. Appendix A details the complete local feature catalog.

3.4 Region Property Prediction 

A natural first approach to representation-level supervision is masked feature modeling (MFM), which predicts local molecular properties at masked positions. However, each target is determined by a short sequence window, much of which remains visible after masking. MFM therefore does not need to integrate evidence beyond that window, making any downstream gain difficult to distinguish from generic local auxiliary regularization. Motivated by this limitation, RPP shifts supervision from local features to tile-level properties defined over genomic regions, encouraging the pooled representation to retain information distributed across a broader sequence context. 

3.4.1 Regional Properties 

For each genomic region, we compute six sequence-derived properties: intrinsic curvature, AT content, melting propensity, G-quadruplex propensity, cruciform propensity, and Z-DNA propensity. Together, these targets span regional geometry, composition, duplex stability, and non-B-DNA structural potential which quantifies sequence-based propensity. Appendix C gives the exact definition, unit, and biological motivation for each selected property. 4VANDAM: viewing a nucleotide sequence with DNA molecular priors

3.5 VANDAM Training Regimes 

The appropriate mechanism for incorporating molecular priors depends on whether the training objective makes the supplied information consequential. We therefore consider two configurations. In the self-supervised pre-training phase, 

VANDAM-PT applies RPP without LFI. We omit LFI because its local descriptors are deterministic functions of context available to the native token objective and are not themselves supervised. In multi-task tuning, when functional task labels are available alongside the native token objective, VANDAM-MT combines LFI with RPP. Task gradients can reward use of the injected descriptors for functional prediction, while RPP directly supervises retention of regional properties.

Evaluation and statistical analysis. We evaluate through GFMBench-API (Larey et al., 2026a) on nine held-out tasks: four zero-shot variant-effect tasks, scored by cosine dissimilarity between pooled reference and alternate-sequence embeddings, and five tasks using frozen representations with linear probes. We use AUROC for variant-effect and classification tasks, and macro Pearson correlation for CAGE expression. Appendix G gives the task sources, sample sizes, metrics, and probe protocol. For each task–backbone comparison, we evaluate the paired score difference ∆ = VANDAM − Baseline ; for linear-probe tasks, this is averaged across five matched probe seeds. We construct 95% confidence intervals from 10,000 paired bootstrap replicates, resampling test units for all tasks and the five probe seeds for linear-probe tasks. Each replicate uses the same sampled test units for VANDAM and Baseline. Zero-shot tasks therefore resample test units only. We obtain one-sided p-values from 10,000 paired randomization replicates that swap VANDAM and Baseline predictions within each test unit; for linear-probe tasks, each swap is shared across seeds. We apply Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) adjustment separately within each training regime and direction. A VANDAM advantage requires a positive mean paired difference, a 95% confidence interval wholly above zero, and an adjusted positive-direction q < 0.05 ; we use the mirrored criterion for Baseline advantages. Appendix H provides the complete inferential procedure and comparison-level results.

## 5 Discussion 

Across four architecture families, including two tokenization schemes, and both MLM and NTP objectives, molecular grounding improves performance in most downstream task comparisons. This consistent performance supports an objective–task alignment interpretation: token recovery is a useful general-purpose pre-training signal, but it does not directly require representations to preserve molecular properties relevant to downstream functional and phenotypic prediction. The pre-training RPP-without-MLM ablation underperforms Baseline-PT (Table 4), further indicating that molecular grounding complements rather than replaces the token objective. This complementarity is best understood through the role of the training objective. VANDAM does not introduce information unavailable from sequence: every local descriptor and regional property used in this work is a deterministic function of the input. The operative variable is instead what the objective obliges the representation to preserve. Making a property available is passive and can be ignored; attaching a loss to it, or attaching labels whose prediction it eases, makes its use consequential. This differs from settings in which a supplied structural prior contains information not directly present in the model’s sequence input, whether that information is experimentally measured or obtained from another source. In the NTv3-8M component ablation, LFI alone matches Baseline-PT during continual pre-training (both 60 .5 mean AUROC), but improves over Baseline-MT during multi-task training ( 64 .2 versus 63 .0; Table 4). This pattern is consistent with a simple asymmetry: the native token objective can be optimized from the same bounded local context from which an injected descriptor is computed, without requiring the representation to retain that descriptor. Functional labels, by contrast, can reward using a biologically meaningful featurization to predict targets not fixed by the local window. Together, the biological-content controls and qDNA probing suggest that RPP does more than make its six supervised targets linearly accessible: it promotes representations that retain broader molecular structure. The persistence of transfer after residualization strengthens this interpretation. 

Limitations and future work. The present study focuses on molecular properties defined over local genomic tiles and on comparatively small backbones with sequence contexts of at most 8,192 bp, enabling controlled compute-matched comparisons across architectures. We therefore do not establish how VANDAM behaves as the spatial scale of its molecular priors, model capacity, and sequence context grow together. Under MLM, zeroing descriptors whose pentamer intersects a mask makes approximately 47% of interior LFI vectors zero. Appendix O evaluates feature-only imputation on NTv3-8M; the effects are task-specific and were not tested across backbones. Extending these masking-policy 8VANDAM: viewing a nucleotide sequence with DNA molecular priors experiments alongside supervision for long-range and three-dimensional genome organization is an important direction for future work. 

## 6 Conclusion 

VANDAM reframes genomic foundation-model training around DNA as a molecule as well as a sequence. By directly supervising regional molecular properties during self-supervised continual pre-training, and by incorporating local molecular descriptors when functional labels can reward their use, it provides objective-appropriate routes for grounding representations in sequence-derived biophysics. Across diverse architectures, tokenizers, and token-identity objectives, these interventions improve downstream genomic representations; RPP also increases access to held-out molecular descriptors. More broadly, molecular priors can help genomic foundation models capture sequence-dependent physical properties of DNA. 9VANDAM: viewing a nucleotide sequence with DNA molecular priors

## B Local-Feature-Injection Alignment and Backbone-Specific Handling 

This section specifies the visibility rules and backbone adaptations for the input-concatenation injector of §3.3. 

Visibility and warm-up. Features are computed from the corrupted sequence available to the backbone, never from the uncorrupted target sequence. For MLM, a masked nucleotide is excluded both at its own position and from the feature windows of neighbouring positions. For NTP, feature windows exclude the target and all future nucleotides. Special tokens receive zero features, and no feature vectors are learned for them. The backbone is frozen while the feature projection Wf and local-feature-injection projection Wm are initialized against its fixed representation; all parameters are then trained jointly. 

BPE token alignment. The features operate at nucleotide resolution, whereas a DNABERT-2 BPE token can span multiple bases. We compute features at base resolution and mean-pool them over the bases spanned by each token. Computing features before pooling preserves context that crosses BPE-token boundaries. Special tokens receive zero features. 

Causal alignment. For causal HyenaDNA, the centered pentamer (xi−2, . . . , x i+2 ) cannot be used at position i,because it includes future bases. We instead use the right-aligned window (xi−4, . . . , x i), which contains only bases xj

with j ≤ i. Thus, the adaptation changes the feature’s determining window, not the position at which it is injected. 

Backbone-specific handling. Caduceus-PS represents forward and reverse-complement strands jointly, so VANDAM applies the same local-feature-injection projection Wm independently to each strand in its natural orientation before restoring the paired representation. This preserves reverse-complement equivariance.

G.2 Prediction Protocols 

Each task is evaluated by first producing a score or prediction and then applying the appropriate evaluation metric. We describe these two steps separately below. 

Zero-shot variant scoring. Reference and alternate sequences are encoded independently using each backbone’s native tokenizer. Their final contextualized token states are pooled by an attention-masked mean to obtain sequence representations href and halt . We score each variant by cosine dissimilarity, si = 1 − cos( h(i)alt , h (i)ref ), so that a larger representational perturbation ranks as more likely to be pathogenic. Nothing is fitted on the evaluation task. Although each sequence pair differs at only one nucleotide, the resulting representation change is not necessarily confined to one hidden state. In single-nucleotide models, the perturbation can propagate across contextualized positions through convolution, attention, or state-space mixing; in DNABERT-2, it may additionally alter the local BPE segmentation. The number and magnitude of affected hidden states are therefore backbone-dependent. Because the reference and alternate sequences share nearly all of their genomic context and are summarized by global mean pooling, however, their sequence-level representations are generally similar. 

Linear probing. For supervised tasks, the backbone is frozen and a single linear head is trained for three epochs with AdamW at learning rate 3 × 10 −5, weight decay 0.01 , and batch size 16. For binary single-sequence tasks, the 17 VANDAM: viewing a nucleotide sequence with DNA molecular priors head maps a pooled representation to a positive-class probability. For the two paired variant-effect tasks, it consumes 

[ halt ∥ href ], allowing an asymmetric comparison. The histone-marks head emits twenty independent sigmoid outputs trained with binary cross-entropy, and the CAGE head maps bin-pooled states to predicted log 1 p counts using MSE. 

G.3 Evaluation Metrics AUROC. AUROC is used for the four zero-shot variant tasks and the binary linear-probe tasks. For the former, it evaluates whether cosine dissimilarity ranks pathogenic above benign variants within each backbone; absolute cosine distances are not compared across backbones. For the latter, it is computed from the predicted positive-class probability. 

Macro AUROC. For histone marks, AUROC is computed independently for each track and then averaged, excluding tracks with only one class present in the test set. Thus, each track contributes equally to the reported score. 

Macro Pearson correlation. For CAGE, all (sequence, bin) pairs are flattened separately for each track, Pearson r is computed per track, and the mean over the fifty tracks is reported. Chance is zero rather than 0.5, which is why this task is never folded into the AUROC mean. 

## H Paired Statistical Inference for Main Results

For each task–backbone comparison, we estimate the paired score difference ∆ = VANDAM − Baseline , reported in the 0–100 units used by the main tables. For the four zero-shot tasks and the two variant-effect probe tasks (eQTL and gene-expression variants), a test unit is one labeled reference–alternate variant pair, including the tissue covariate for eQTL. ## M Biological-Content Controls 

This section specifies the matched controls used in Figure 2. Each control uses the same backbone, data, and training budget as its biological comparison; the intervention is the content of the injected features or RPP targets. 

Local-feature-injection control. For the LFI (rand) control, the biological local descriptor vector ϕ(x)i is replaced by a random vector of the same dimension at every position. This leaves the injector’s parameter count and gradient path unchanged while removing the sequence-derived molecular signal. 

RPP target controls. The shuffled-table control applies one fixed permutation to the rows of the pentamer shape table and recomputes the same regional properties through the same estimators. It therefore preserves the targets’ marginal distributions, cross-feature correlations, and order sensitivity, while destroying the pentamer-to-shape correspondence. The algorithmic control instead predicts three generic regional sequence statistics with no molecular interpretation: regional compression ratio, third-order entropy rate, and minimizer density. Two of these depend on base arrangement rather than composition alone, preserving the regional integration requirement of RPP while removing the biological sequence-to-property mapping. 

Multi-task configurations. LFI (rand) + RPP retains biological RPP while randomizing the injected descriptors. LFI (bio) + RPP (shuf) and LFI (bio) + RPP (alg) retain biological injected descriptors while replacing only the RPP targets. Thus, each multi-task comparison isolates the content of one biological pathway while holding the remaining architecture and training setup fixed.

## O Sensitivity to Masked-Base Handling in LFI 

The reported LFI policy sets a feature vector to zero whenever its determining pentamer contains an explicit ⟨mask ⟩

token, preventing the feature stream from revealing a masked nucleotide. Under the 15% MLM masking rate and standard 80/10/10 replacement policy, a nucleotide is replaced by ⟨mask ⟩ with probability 0.15 × 0.8 = 0 .12 . An interior pentamer therefore contains at least one mask with probability 1 − (1 − 0.12) 5 ≈ 47 .2% , making the injected feature stream sparse during MLM training. We evaluated two leakage-safe alternatives on NTv3-8M VANDAM-MT. Both construct a separate completed sequence used only to compute DNAshape features; the backbone still receives the original corrupted sequence. Uniform replaces each masked base with A, C, G, or T with equal probability. Conditional samples it from the OpenGenome2 pentamer distribution conditioned on the visible pentamer context, with pseudocount smoothing. Replacements are resampled during training and applied simultaneously, so adjacent masks introduce no ordering effect. A single replacement can restore features for up to five overlapping pentamers.
